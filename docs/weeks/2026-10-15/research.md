## Huang's research: emotion support in AI chat

Prepared on October 22, 2026, for that evening's meeting. This work belongs to the October 15 research cycle.

### Main findings

- Evidence-based practice (EBP) combines research, clinical experience, and patient needs and preferences. CBT is one therapy approach within this broader idea (S007).
- Pause Lab's emotion EBP program offers ideas for emotion awareness, reflection, mindfulness, and small actions (S009).
- Its 2025 study supports further investigation of the program. It does not establish that an AI chat version would work; the randomized trial excluded people receiving counseling or other psychological interventions (S008).
- Auralink offers a between-session AI chat and therapist summaries. Its product description is a reference, not evidence that our proposed system is effective (S010).

### Ideas for our prototype

- Start with what the patient wants to discuss. Offer exercises when useful, and allow skipping, changing topics, or stopping.
- Let patients resume earlier conversations and correct short summaries. Measure usefulness and ease of returning, rather than chat length or daily streaks.
- Let therapists review original messages and summaries. Clearly identify therapist participation and pause AI guidance during a therapist takeover.
- Keep crisis alerts as a separate workflow requiring clinical agreement. Privacy, access, and retention policies remain unresolved.

These are design proposals, not approved clinical requirements. First evaluate fictional conversations with professional reviewers for accuracy, patient control, and therapist usefulness. Clinical benefit and continued use need separate evaluation.
